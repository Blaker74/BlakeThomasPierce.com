Stories behind the stories

This journal is a growing home for original writing about memory, craft, working life and the experiences that find their way into fiction.

October 23, 2026

Why ordinary lives belong in books

Some stories announce themselves. Most do not. Most arrive disguised as everyday life: somebody coming home tired from work, a family laughing at an old saying, a car that should have quit years ago, a house nobody else would think twice about.

Those details can look too small to matter while we are living them. Years later they become the things we would give almost anything to see one more time.

That is one reason I write about working people and families. A life does not become meaningful because it happened in a famous place or to a famous person. Meaning comes from what people gave, endured, remembered and loved.

October 23, 2026

Writing from memory without turning memory into a museum

Memory is useful to a storyteller because it keeps strange things. It may forget an entire month and preserve the sound of a screen door, the color of a wall or one sentence somebody said forty years ago.

But fiction has to become more than a list of memories. The details have to serve the people in the story. They have to create a place the reader can enter rather than a private museum only the writer understands.

For me, the test is simple: does the detail help a reader feel something even if they never lived my life? If it does, it belongs to the story.

October 23, 2026

What work teaches a writer

Decades around heavy-duty trucks taught me that people rarely explain a problem in perfect language. You listen, ask questions, notice what does not fit and keep working until the pieces make sense.

Writing is not completely different. A first draft tells you something is wrong long before it tells you exactly how to fix it. Characters resist the plan. Scenes that looked good in an outline can sit dead on the page. You keep listening to the story until you understand what it actually needs.

The job is not to make the work look effortless. The job is to stay with the problem long enough to make the finished result feel inevitable.

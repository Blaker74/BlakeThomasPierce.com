A Novel by Blake Pierce

The Words Between Us

When words disappear, a son refuses to accept that communication must disappear with them.

A family story at the edge of possibility

Seventeen-year-old Raven grows up on remote family land in Alaska, surrounded by distance, weather, machinery and the expectations of a life his father understands well. Raven respects that life, but his restless curiosity keeps pulling him toward a wider world.

After an opportunity takes him to Seattle, everything changes when his mother, Alina, suffers a stroke. She remains aware of the people she loves but can no longer form understandable words. For Raven, the problem becomes intensely personal: if the mind still knows what it wants to say, can technology find another path for those words?

At the heart of the novel: NOVA—Neural Output Vocal Assistance—begins not as a quest for fame, but as a son's attempt to hear his mother communicate again.

Technology with a human reason to exist

The invention grows through failure, experimentation and the discovery that meaningful emotional connection can strengthen the signal Raven is trying to understand. The science-fiction element opens the door, but family remains at the center of the room.

The story explores communication, ambition, home, grief, love and what happens when an invention becomes larger than the private reason it was created.

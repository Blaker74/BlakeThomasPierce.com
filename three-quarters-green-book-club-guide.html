Reading Group Guide

Three-Quarters Green Book Club Guide & Discussion Questions

Questions for book clubs, reading groups and anyone who finishes the last page and wants to keep talking.

Three-Quarters Green follows Lake Farmer from a childhood in a twelve-by-sixty mobile home, painted three-quarters green and never quite finished, through work, marriage, children, moves, losses and new beginnings. It is a story about where we come from, who walks beside us and the moments we do not realize are precious until years later.

The questions below are built around the book's big themes, so a group can use them in any order. Pick the ones that fit your conversation and skip the rest.

A note on spoilers: The first section is safe for groups still reading. The later sections assume everyone has finished the book.

Before you finish: starting the conversation

  1. The title comes from a mobile home that is three-quarters painted green. What does an unfinished house say about the family living in it? Did the image change for you as the story went on?
  2. Lake grows up in humble circumstances, but the book does not ask readers to pity him. How does the story handle being poor without turning it into the whole point?
  3. Which detail from Lake's childhood (an object, a place, a saying, a job) brought back a memory of your own?
  4. Resourcefulness shows up again and again: figuring out a problem with whatever is on hand. Where did you notice it, and who in your own life worked that way?

Family and the people who form us

  1. Which family member or friend shaped Lake the most? Was that the same person you expected when the story began?
  2. Family sayings and habits get passed down in the book. What sayings were repeated in your house growing up, and do you still hear yourself using them?
  3. Imperfect families are treated as worth remembering. How does the novel balance love and frustration, pride and hurt?

Work and working-class life

  1. Early jobs and long days are part of Lake's world. How does work shape who he becomes, beyond earning a living?
  2. The author spent decades in the heavy-duty truck industry before publishing fiction. Do you think a working life gives a writer something that is hard to get any other way?
  3. Working people are often background characters in fiction. What changes when they are placed at the center of the story?

Memory, loss and time

  1. The book suggests that a life does not have to be famous to matter. Do you agree? Which ordinary moment in the story felt the most important by the end?
  2. How does the story handle loss? Did a particular passage stay with you after you put the book down?
  3. Small memories can become the ones we would give anything to live once more. If you could step back into one ordinary day from your own past for five minutes, which would it be?
  4. As Lake grows older, the scenery changes but the heart of the story stays close to home. What does "home" mean by the end of the book?

Reading wider

  1. What other books about rural childhood, family or working-class America would you pair with Three-Quarters Green? Why?
  2. The novel draws on the texture of a real Southeast Missouri childhood but is told as fiction. Why might a writer choose a novel over a memoir to tell a story like this?
Going further: Blake has written about the real trailer childhood in rural Missouri behind the book, and about how real memories become fiction. Both make good companion reading for a group discussion.
